# What tips, room by room

Across the US, CPSC counts about 17,800-22,500 emergency-room visits a year from furniture, TVs and
appliances tipping over. Of 581 tip-over deaths from 2000 to 2021, 81% were children, and TVs were
involved in most of the children's deaths. Children and older adults are the most at risk.

## Nursery and children's rooms
- Dressers, chests and changing tables: toddlers open the drawers and climb them like stairs.
  Most child deaths from furniture involve these.
- A TV on a dresser: the TV falls first, then the dresser if the child keeps climbing.
- Bookcases and cube shelves full of toys at child height: climbing to reach the top.
- Wardrobes: a child hangs on an open door.
- Who: ages 1-6 climb. A crib, a toddler bed or a play mat in front of a piece is where it lands.

## Adult bedrooms
- Dressers and chests, often with a TV on top. Wardrobes. Nightstands are low but light.
- Leaning floor mirrors.
- Who: visiting grandchildren; older adults steadying themselves on the way to the bathroom at night.

## Living rooms
- TVs on stands, consoles and dressers; media units with doors.
- Bookcases, display or curio cabinets, often with glass.
- Tall floor lamps, plants in tall pots, standing mirrors.
- Who: children reaching for a remote or a game console; adults leaning on a cabinet.

## Kitchens and dining rooms
- Freestanding ranges: a child standing on the open oven door. The anti-tip bracket is the fix.
- Hutches and china cabinets: heavy and top-loaded with dishes, with glass doors.
- Freestanding pantry shelving and bakers' racks, and microwaves on carts.
- Built-in and wall-hung cabinets don't tip: leave them out.

## Home offices
- Filing cabinets: several loaded drawers open at once pull them over. Office units often have
  interlocks.
- Bookcases, shelving units, tall storage cabinets.

## Garages, workshops and basements
- Steel shelving loaded at the top, rolling tool chests with drawers open, tall cabinets, gun safes.
- Who: children exploring; adults pulling a heavy drawer.

## Bathrooms, laundry rooms and hallways
- Freestanding linen towers and over-toilet shelves.
- Stacked washer and dryer without a stacking kit.
- Shoe cabinets with tilt-out drawers: very shallow, so they tip easily. Free-standing coat racks.

## Classrooms and daycares
- Cubbies, bookshelves, and TVs on carts or high stands.

## Homes of older adults
- Anything grabbed for support between bed, bathroom and chair: dressers, bookcases, cabinets.
- Shallow or light pieces next to a walking path matter most.

## Earthquake regions
- Everything tall or top-heavy, and anything with drawers or doors that can fly open: latches and
  wall straps matter here even for adults-only homes. Upper floors shake harder than the ground floor.
