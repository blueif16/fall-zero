# Other things that fall (judged, not simulated)

These aren't boxes with drawers, doors or shelves, so the engine can't build them. Judge them with
the three risks and give practical fixes.

## Leaning floor mirrors and standing mirrors
- Full-length mirrors leaned against a wall (15-30 kg, glass) slide or tip forward when a child pulls
  on the frame or a pet brushes them. Glass makes every fall serious.
- Child: tips unless fixed to the wall. Adult: maybe, if leaned on. Quake: tips.
- Fixes: anchor the top to the wall (a strap or mirror clips), or hang it.

## Tall floor lamps and torchieres
- Light (3-8 kg) on a narrow base: they topple easily but rarely do serious harm unless glass shades
  or hot bulbs are involved.
- Child: maybe. Adult: maybe if grabbed. Quake: maybe.
- Fixes: move it out of play areas and walking paths; use LED bulbs.

## Coat racks and hall trees
- Free-standing coat racks tip when loaded on one side or pulled by a child; hall trees with a bench
  are heavier but top-loaded.
- Fixes: anchor to the wall, keep the heavy coats low and even.

## Gun safes and heavy cabinets on plinths
- 100-400 kg, tall and shallow for their weight. They should be bolted to the floor: an unbolted safe
  that tips is deadly, though it takes a large force to start.
- Fixes: bolt to the floor (and wall); ask whether it is bolted.

## Tall decor and plants
- Plants in tall pots (a 30 L pot with wet soil is 30-40 kg), sculptures and pedestals tip when
  climbed or pulled.
- Fixes: move heavy pots off pedestals; anchor tall decor.

## Wall-mounted shelves and TV mounts
- Already fixed to the wall, so they don't tip, but a shelf loaded past its anchors or a TV mount
  in drywall without studs can pull out. Judge only when the photo shows a problem: sagging, gaps
  at the wall, or a large TV on a small mount.
- Fixes: re-mount into studs with the right anchors; reduce the load.

## Baby and child gear
- High chairs, bouncers and play yards fall over with the child in them. That is a different hazard
  (product stability with a child inside). Mention it only when it is clearly at risk, for example a
  high chair next to a counter the child can push off.
